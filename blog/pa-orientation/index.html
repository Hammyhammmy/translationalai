Start here: Three Geometries of Failure
A 10-minute read to do first. Body structures come in three shapes: layers, conduits (tubes) and networks. Each shape gives you one question that points to where the problem really is. Every other page builds on this.

Conduits — The Urinary Tract

The urinary tract is our worked example of a conduit: urine is made, carried, stored and let out through five stations, kidney to urethra. For any urology problem, ask "which station is blocked or broken, and what has backed up above it?"

UT
The Urinary Tract — A Conduit
Open this first in urology. The five stations from kidney to urethra, how the bladder switches between filling and emptying, what goes wrong at each station, which test to order for each, and a worked case of a man who can't pass urine because of an enlarged prostate.
→
IA
The Urinary Tract — Interactive Model
A live picture of the whole system on real anatomy. Watch urine being made and flowing out, switch the bladder between filling and emptying and see which nerves do each, then pick any of ~30 conditions and watch what breaks: swelling above the problem, nothing coming through below it.
→
CN
Common Consults — ER & Inpatient
The nine urology calls you'll get in the ER and on the wards, each laid out the same way: where the problem is, what you'll see, what to order, what to do, and when to call. Which ones can't wait, clear rules for when to wake the urologist, and a worked case of a patient who can't void the day after surgery.
→
PR
Procedures the Trainee Will Do
Hands-on skills you'll be asked to do: placing a Foley catheter (and what to try when it won't pass), needle drainage of the bladder, collecting urine, setting up bladder irrigation, checking a urostomy. For each: when to do it, how, what goes wrong, and when to get help.
→
MD
Medications & Their Stations
The urology drugs you'll see on med lists: α-blockers, 5-α reductase inhibitors, bladder relaxants (antimuscarinics, β3 agonists), PDE5 inhibitors, antibiotics. Each one tied to the part of the tract it works on and how, so you can work out why a patient is on it instead of memorizing a list.
→
OP
The Operations — What Actually Happens
TURP, HoLEP, ureteroscopy, robotic prostatectomy, partial nephrectomy, in plain terms. For each: which part of the tract it works on, what it removes or rebuilds, what recovery looks like day by day, and the complications you'll get called about.
→
FU
Clinic Follow-Up Patterns
The repeat clinic visits: following a PSA, checking for bladder cancer coming back, a kidney mass found by chance, recurrent UTI in women vs men. Plus a clinic note template, kidney to urethra, that tells the next person what to do.

Deep Dives & Decision Tools

Pages that each answer one question in more depth than the overviews above: one decision, the evidence behind it, and where that evidence is thin.

SRM
The 2–4 cm Renal Mass
Who gets pushed to treatment and who stays on surveillance. Five forks for a solid, enhancing cT1a mass, worked example cases, and the DISSRM, MSKCC and SEER numbers behind each lean, with source receipts.

PBx
Prostate Biopsy Risk — calculator
PCPTRC 2.0, PBCG and the MRI-based TUM-RC side by side: enter the patient once and compare high-grade risk across models. Runs in the page; nothing is sent anywhere.

Stones in Pregnancy

Layers — Planned

The geometry where defects sit at depth and present at the surface. Other specialties that fit: dermatology (epidermis → dermis → subcutaneous), neurosurgery (meninges), thoracic surgery (chest wall planes).

AW
The Abdominal Wall — Hernia Anatomy
The original layer-pattern case study. Skin → fascia → muscle → transversalis → peritoneum. Inguinal, femoral, umbilical, incisional. Finding the gap in the fascia by exam alone.
→
SK
Skin & Soft Tissue — A Layered Read
Cellulitis, abscess, necrotizing fasciitis, eschar. Which layer the infection is in determines whether antibiotics will reach it.
→

Networks — Planned

The geometry where one source supplies a fan of dependent territories. Failure of a branch is read by what's missing downstream. Cardiology, neurology, vascular surgery, and parts of pulmonology all fit here.

VT
Vascular Trees — Stroke & Ischemia
Territory reading. MCA vs ACA vs PCA stroke. Coronary territories on the ECG. Peripheral arterial disease by Rutherford class. The deficit defines the branch.
→
PN
Peripheral Nerves — Dermatomes & Myotomes
Radiculopathy as a territory map. Plexus injuries. The motor/sensory distribution tells you the level. The history tells you the mechanism.
