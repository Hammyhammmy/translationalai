What it is

One pile in. Three usable things out.

A referral arrives as whatever the sender had: a fax, a scanned bundle, a predecessor's notes, three reports stapled into one PDF. Today someone reads all of it before the visit — or nobody does, and the physician meets the patient cold.

ChartPrepper reads the pile and prepares the chart. Not a transcript, not a rewrite: a structured summary, a questionnaire ready to send to the patient, and a set of suggestions to consider before the visit.

Output 01

Summary

Reason for referral, problems, medications, allergies, labs, imaging — pulled out of the documents and laid out the way a chart is read, with every line traceable.

Output 02

AI intake

A questionnaire built from what the chart is missing. The patient or the office fills it in before the visit; the answers arrive as part of the chart, marked as patient-reported.

Output 03

AI colleague

A curbside opinion on the prepared chart — questions to ask, things worth ruling out. Suggestions the physician takes or drops; nothing is added to the note unsupervised.

How it holds up

Every line carries its source.

A summary a physician has to re-check against the original saves nobody any time. So the unit of work isn't the sentence — it's the sentence plus where it came from. Each extracted statement is anchored to the exact quote in the source document and the page it sits on, and that quote is checked to actually be there.

When the anchor holds, the line carries a citation you can open beside the summary. When it doesn't, the line isn't quietly published — it comes back flagged for a human to confirm.

Provenance
Statement → exact quote → page. Merged from several documents, each line keeps the [1] of the document it came from.
Flags, not guesses
A claim that can't be sourced comes back as [VERIFY] with the reason, and stays flagged until a human marks it ✓ verified.
Calibration
Extraction and note prompts are graded against gold-standard charts, so referral chatter doesn't end up in the problem list. Fluent is easy; correct is the work.
Data residency
Built for PIPEDA and PHIPA: processing stays in Canada (Montreal), and raw PDFs are ephemeral — read, summarized, deleted. Only the structured, cited chart persists.
During the visit

An ambient scribe

Captures the visit you are in. Its data is the conversation happening now, and there is no source document to cite.

Before the visit

ChartPrepper

Digests everything that came before the visit — the record pile — and cites it. A different moment, different data, and provenance a scribe has nothing to point at.

Who builds the chart

The prep stops being one person's job.

The chart that used to be assembled by whoever had time is now something several people add to, each in their own lane and each on the record. The patient answers the intake. The office uploads the pile and runs the prep. The physician reads, corrects, and signs.

What each of them contributed is stamped on the chart itself — and the byline only ever names contributors the chart actually records. It never asserts a physician touched a chart they haven't.

The product isn't the summary. It's a chart a physician trusts without re-reading the source.
